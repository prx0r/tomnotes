Episode: milinda_001_chariot_deconstruction Nāgasena: If, sire, you walk at noon-time on the hot scorching ground, trampling on sharp grit, pebbles and sand, your feet will hurt you, your body will be tired, your mind will be disturbed and you will suffer bodily pain. Now, did you come on foot or in a vehicle? King Milinda: I, revered sir, did not come on foot. I came in a chariot. Nāgasena: If, sire, you came in a chariot, show me the chariot. Is the pole the chariot, sire? King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Is the axle the chariot? King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Are the wheels the chariot? King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Then, sire, are the pole, the axle, the wheels, the body, the flag-staff, the yoke, the reins, the goad all together the chariot? King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Well then, sire, is the chariot apart from the pole, the axle, the wheels, the body, the flag-staff, the yoke, the reins and the goad? King Milinda: No, sire. Nāgasena: Sire, though I have been asking you repeatedly, I do not see this chariot. Chariot is only a sound. What then is the chariot you say you came in? You, sire, have spoken a falsehood, an untruth. There is no chariot. King Milinda: I am not, revered sir, speaking a lie. It is because of the pole, because of the axle, the wheels, the body, the flag-staff, the yoke, the reins and because of the goad that ‘chariot’ exists as a mere designation. Nāgasena: It is good, sire, that you understand a chariot. Even so is it for me, sire, because of the hairs of the head … because of the urine, because of material form … because of consciousness, that ‘Nāgasena’ exists as a mere designation. However, in the ultimate sense there is no person as such that is found.

Episode: milinda_002_speech_of_kings King Milinda: Revered Nāgasena, will you converse with me? Nāgasena: I will converse if you, sire, will converse in the speech of the learned, but if you converse in the speech of kings I will not converse. King Milinda: How, revered Nāgasena, do the learned converse? Nāgasena: When the learned are conversing, sire, there is a turning over and an unravelling of the subject; then there is a refutation and an acknowledgement of a mistake; distinctions and contradistinctions are drawn; yet, thereby, they are not angered. It is thus, sire, that the learned converse. King Milinda: And how, revered sir, do kings converse? Nāgasena: When kings are conversing, sire, they approve of some matter and order punishment for anyone who disagrees with that matter, saying: ‘Inflict a punishment on him.’ It is thus, sire, that kings converse. King Milinda: I, revered sir, will converse in the speech of the learned, not in the speech of kings. Let the revered one converse unreservedly as he converses with a monk or a novice or a lay follower or with a monastery attendant. Let the revered one converse thus, let him not be afraid.

Episode: milinda_003_identity_lamp_baby King Milinda: Revered Nāgasena, is he who uprises the same or is he another? Nāgasena: Neither the same nor another. What do you think about this, sire? Now that you are grown up are you the same as you were when you were a boy, young, tender, lying on your back? King Milinda: No, revered sir. That boy, young, tender, lying on his back was one thing. I, who am now full-grown, am another. Nāgasena: That being so, sire, you can have no mother, nor can you have a father... It is one ‘I’ that was the boy, young, tender, lying on his back and it is the same ‘I’ that am now full-grown, and all these are held together as a unity in dependence on this body itself. King Milinda: Make a simile. Nāgasena: It is as if, sire, some person might light a lamp. Would it burn all night long? King Milinda: Yes, revered sir, it might burn all night long. Nāgasena: Is the flame of the first watch of the night the same as the flame of the middle watch? King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Is the flame of the middle watch the same as the flame of the third watch? King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Is it then, sire, that the lamp in the first watch was one thing, the lamp in the middle watch another, and the lamp in the third watch still another? King Milinda: No, revered sir, it was burning all through the night in dependence on itself. Nāgasena: Even so, sire, a continuity of phenomena is linked together. It uprises as one and ceases as another, linked together as though there were no before and no after. Hence it is neither the same nor another, but the subsequent consciousness is reckoned (together with) the preceding consciousness.

Episode: milinda_004_mango_thief_karma King Milinda: Revered Nāgasena, who takes rebirth? Nāgasena: Mind-and-matter, sire, takes rebirth. King Milinda: What, is it this same mind-and-matter itself that takes rebirth? Nāgasena: This same mind-and-matter does not itself take rebirth, sire; but, sire, by means of this mind-and-matter one does a good or bad deed and, because of this deed, another mind-and-matter takes rebirth. King Milinda: If, revered sir, this mind-and-matter itself does not take rebirth would one not be freed from evil deeds? Nāgasena: If, sire, it did not take rebirth one would be freed from evil deeds. But because, sire, it takes rebirth one is not freed from evil deeds. ... Suppose, sire, some man were to steal another man’s mangoes and the owner of the mangoes were to seize him and bring him before the king... and he were to say, ‘Your majesty, I did not steal his mangoes; the mangoes he planted were different from these that I stole; I do not deserve punishment.’ Would he, sire, not deserve punishment? King Milinda: Yes, revered sir, he would deserve punishment. Nāgasena: For what reason? King Milinda: Whatever he may say, the mangoes he stole resulted from the mangoes which, he admits, the other man planted. Nāgasena: Even so, sire, by means of this mind-and-matter one does a good or bad deed and, because of this deed, another mind-and-matter takes rebirth; therefore, one is not freed from evil deeds.

Episode: milinda_005_soul_window_homunculus King Milinda: Revered Nāgasena, is there such a thing as an experiencer? Nāgasena: What does this ‘experiencer’ mean, sire? King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Again, sire, we who are sitting here in the palace will see visible forms out there before us more clearly in the great space if these network windows are removed. King Milinda: No, revered sir. Nāgasena: Because, sire, of the eye and visible form eye-consciousness arises. ... Thus these things are produced from a condition and there is no experiencer found here.

Episode: milinda_006_slope_consciousness King Milinda: Revered Nāgasena, where eye-consciousness arises, does mind-consciousness also arise there? Nāgasena: Yes, sire, mind-consciousness also arises there. King Milinda: It would take the same course as the earlier water. Nāgasena: Then, sire, does the earlier water instruct the later, saying ‘You run away by the same course as I do,’ or does the later water instruct the earlier, saying ‘I will also run away by the same course as you will take’? King Milinda: No, revered sir, there is no conversation between them; they run away because there is a slope. Nāgasena: Even so, sire, it is because of tendency that where eye-consciousness arises, mind-consciousness arises there too.

Episode: milinda_011_mindfulness_treasurer King Milinda: Revered Nāgasena, what is the distinguishing mark of mindfulness? King Milinda: Make a simile. Nāgasena: As, sire, the adviser-treasurer of a Wheel-turning King knows what is beneficial and what is detrimental for the king and thinks: ‘These are beneficial for the king, these are detrimental’... and then removes what is detrimental and takes up what is beneficial... even so, sire, when mindfulness is uprising one examines the qualities of mental states... and takes up helpful mental states.
